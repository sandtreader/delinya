Lines, rectangles, circles and paths each have a line style: a pattern
and a pen width. Text has none.

## Choosing a style

The **line style** group in the toolbar has four pattern buttons, **Solid**,
**Dashed**, **Dotted** and **Dash-dot** (a centre line), and a **width**
menu.

<Screenshot src={toolbar} alt="The line style group from the toolbar: Solid (pressed), Dashed, Dotted and Dash-dot buttons, and the width menu showing 0.35 mm" />

- With nothing selected, it shows and sets the style for the shapes you
  draw next. Your choice is remembered in your browser.
- With shapes selected, it shows theirs ("mixed" where they differ), and
  a choice restyles them as well as setting the style for what you draw
  next.

<Screenshot src={patterns} alt="Four lines at 0.35 mm, top to bottom: solid, dashed, dotted and dash-dot" />

## Widths are pen widths

The widths are the standard technical pen sizes: 0.13, 0.18, 0.25, 0.35
(the default), 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.4 and 2 mm. They are widths **on paper**: a
0.5 mm line prints 0.5 mm wide at 1:1 or at 1:10, and looks the same
thickness on screen at any zoom.

<Screenshot src={widths} alt="Five solid lines, each labelled with its width: 0.18, 0.35, 0.7, 1.4 and 2 mm, from hairline to bold" />

The dash lengths follow the ISO standard, in proportion to the width,
but no shorter than for 0.35 mm, so thin lines still look dashed.

## In output

Printing, PDF and SVG use the widths and patterns. DXF keeps the
patterns as linetypes (DASHED, DOTTED, DASHDOT), but has no line widths.
