Text goes in boxes. A box's **height sets the font size** and its
**width sets where lines wrap**; the text is never stretched.

## Adding text

Press **T** (Text) and drag out a box. You're straight into typing on the
drawing; the box grows downwards as lines are added. To finish, press
**Escape** or **⌘Enter** (Ctrl+Enter), or click somewhere else. A new box
left empty disappears without trace.

To edit a box later, **double-click** it, or select it and press **Enter**
or **F2**. Emptying a box and finishing deletes it (one Undo brings it
back).

## The text panel

With a text box selected, the panel has:

- **text**: the text itself, as an alternative to typing on the drawing.
  Enter starts a new line.
- **font**: Sans, Serif or Mono.
- **size**: the font size in mm. Changing it changes the box's height to
  fit.
- **style**: Bold and Italic, for the whole box.
- **align**: Left, Centre or Right.
- x, y, width, height and **rotation**, as for a rectangle.
- **mirrored**: reads backwards, as flipping does, but in place. Useful
  for text seen from the other side, or transferred face down.

A box is never narrower than its longest word, so aligned lines line up
on the word's end rather than spilling out of the box.

## Fonts

The three fonts are the standard PDF ones: Helvetica, Times and Courier.
PDFs print exactly as measured; SVG names common equivalents (Arial,
Liberation Sans and so on) and stretches the spacing to fit, and DXF
writes each line as text. While you type on the drawing, your browser's
own font is used, so a line may wrap slightly differently until you
finish.
