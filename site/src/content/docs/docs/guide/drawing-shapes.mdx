Pick a tool in the tool palette, or press its key:

| Tool | Key | Draw by |
|---|---|---|
| Line | L | dragging from one end to the other |
| Rectangle | R | dragging from one corner to the opposite one |
| Circle | C | dragging from the centre out to the radius |
| Path | P | clicking node by node: see [Paths](/docs/guide/paths/) |
| Text | T | dragging out a box: see [Text](/docs/guide/text/) |

After drawing, Delinya goes back to the Select tool and selects the new
shape, so you can type its exact size in the panel straight away. Hold
**Shift** as you release to stay in the same tool and draw another.

Holding **Shift** while dragging keeps a rectangle square and a line at
multiples of 45°.

## Snapping

The pointer snaps to other shapes' key points (ends, corners, midpoints,
centres and a circle's quarter points) within a few pixels, and otherwise
to the grid. The status bar
says what it snapped to.

<Screenshot src={snap} alt="A line being drawn towards a rectangle, its end snapped to the middle of the rectangle's top edge, which is marked with a small orange square" />

The grid's spacing belongs to the drawing (1 mm unless you change it in
**Properties… → Grid**). Showing the grid and snapping to it are your own
settings, also in Properties: with grid snap off, points round to
0.01 mm, but still snap to other shapes. Zoomed out, the grid shows every
2nd, 5th or 10th line so it never gets too dense.

<Screenshot src={gridSettings} alt="Properties, on the Grid tab: Spacing 1 mm, with Show grid and Snap to grid both ticked" />

## Exact sizes

The selection panel shows the selected shape's position and sizes in
millimetres: x and y (a rectangle's top-left corner, a line's start),
then width and height for a rectangle, length and angle for a line, and
for a circle cx and cy (its centre) and its diameter. Type a value and press
Enter or Tab. Angles are in degrees, clockwise on screen from pointing
right.

A rectangle also has a **rotation** and **corner radii**: see
[Rotate and duplicate](/docs/guide/rotate-and-duplicate/) and
[Dimensions](/docs/guide/dimensions/).

## Names

Every shape has a name. Until you give it one it goes by its id, a code
like `rect_bt5a44hq`, which the **name** field at the top of the panel
shows greyed out: type over it to name the shape. Once it has a name,
tick **show** to write it in the middle of the shape (just above the
middle of a line), in its layer's colour, on screen and in print, PDF and
SVG (Print / Export's **Shape names** option can leave them out); DXF
leaves names out. Clearing the name stops it showing. Text boxes
have names but don't show them, since their text is already there.

A name is just a label: dimensions and links don't depend on it, so
renaming a shape changes nothing else.

## Where new shapes go

New shapes go on the current layer (see [Layers](/docs/guide/layers/))
in the current line style (see [Line styles](/docs/guide/line-styles/)).
With **Dimension new shapes** on in the toolbar, a new rectangle gets its
width and height dimensions, a circle its diameter and a line its length.
