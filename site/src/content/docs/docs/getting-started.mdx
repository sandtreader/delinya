Open **[delinya.net](https://delinya.net)**. You start with an empty,
untitled drawing on a millimetre grid.

## Finding your way around

- **The toolbar** along the top: New, Open…, Save and Save as…;
  Properties…, Collaborate… and Print / Export…; Undo and Redo; Rotate…
  and Duplicate…; Dimension new shapes; and the line style. The file name
  sits at the right-hand end, with a • while there are unsaved changes.
- **The tool palette**, floating at the top left: Select (V), Line (L),
  Rectangle (R), Circle (C), Path (P), Text (T) and Dimension (D). Drag
  its grip to move it.
- **The layer palette** below it: one numbered button per layer.
- **The selection panel** at the top right, when something is selected:
  its exact position and size, and Delete.
- **The rulers** along the top and left, in millimetres.
- **The status bar** along the bottom: where the pointer is, or the size
  of what you're drawing or moving; then the zoom (px/mm), Reset view,
  Fit to drawing, Show dimensions and Full screen.

Scroll or pinch to zoom at the pointer. Hold Space and drag, or drag with
the middle mouse button, to pan.

## A first drawing

1. Press **R** (or pick Rectangle in the tool palette) and drag out a
   rectangle. It snaps to the grid, and gets its width and height
   dimensions. The status bar shows its size as you drag.
2. In the selection panel, type the sizes you want: say width 600 and
   height 400. Press Enter or Tab to apply a value, Escape to go back to
   the old one.
3. Press **C** and drag from where a hole's centre goes, out to its
   radius. Type its diameter in the panel.
4. Click a shape with the Select tool (**V**) to select it again; drag it
   to move it, or drag its handles to resize it. **⌘Z** (Ctrl+Z) undoes.
5. Press **F** to fit the drawing to the window.

## Save and print

- **Save** (⌘S) names the file the first time; drawings are `.dln` files.
- **Print / Export…** (⌘P) previews the sheets. Choose the paper and
  scale, then **Print…** or **Download PDF**. At 1:1 a drawing bigger than
  the paper is tiled across sheets, with overlap guides to tape them
  together; turn on the 100 mm check ruler to confirm the printer's scale.
