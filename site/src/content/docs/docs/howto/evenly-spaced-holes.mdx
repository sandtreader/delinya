A cabinet side with a column of shelf-pin holes: 5 mm holes at a 32 mm
pitch, 37 mm in from the front edge. The same steps work for any row of
holes, slots or parts.

## The panel

1. Press **R** and drag out a rectangle. In the panel, type **x** 0,
   **y** 0, **width** 300 and **height** 600.

Starting the panel at 0, 0 makes the holes' positions easy to type.

## The first hole

2. Press **C** and drag out a small circle anywhere.
3. In the panel, type **cx** 37, **cy** 96 and **diameter** 5.

## The rest of the column

4. With the hole selected, click **Duplicate…** in the toolbar.
5. Set the direction to **down** (↓) and **×** to the number of extra
   holes, say 12.
6. Set the **gap**. It's the clear space between holes, not the pitch:
   for a 32 mm pitch with 5 mm holes, the gap is 32 − 5 = **27**.
7. Tick the **link** boxes:
   - **gaps** keeps the holes evenly spaced between the first and the
     last;
   - **size** gives them all one diameter, so changing one changes all;
   - **align** keeps them in a straight column.
8. Click **Duplicate**.

Now drag the last hole (or type its **cy**) and the ones between spread
out evenly. Change any hole's diameter and they all follow.

## A second column

9. Lasso all the holes, then in the Duplicate card set the direction to
   **right**, **×** to 1 and the **gap** to the distance you want between
   the columns, less one hole's diameter. Untick the links if this copy
   should move on its own.

## Already drawn?

If the holes are already there, select them all and use **Space →
Centres ↕** in the panel: the outermost two stay put and the rest are
spaced at an equal pitch between them. Turn on **Keep linked** first to
keep them even as you move the outer holes. See
[Links](/docs/guide/links/).
